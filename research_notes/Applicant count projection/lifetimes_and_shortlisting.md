# Posting lifetimes, screening timing and who a late applicant really competes with

Research date: 2026-10-11. Sources are labelled **[STUDY]** (large dataset or academic, method stated), **[VENDOR]** (product company's own data or claims, method partly or wholly unpublished), **[PLATFORM RULE]** (official documentation), **[ANECDOTE]** (blogs, recruiter quotes, secondhand citations).

## 1. Typical posting duration, distribution, time-to-fill and time-to-first-interview

### Takeaway
On ATS career sites a typical posting is live about 1 to 2 weeks (median 8 to 14 days on Lever, Greenhouse and Ashby). LinkedIn and Indeed postings show about 30 days because those sites close listings automatically at expiry, not because the employer acted, so their stated age overstates how long the employer is really looking. Applications arrive heavily front-loaded (about 41% in 48 h, 56% in 96 h). Time-to-fill is about 4 to 10 weeks, and the first interview typically comes about 1 week after applying.

### Cited Findings
**Posting lifetimes**
- [STUDY, aggregator dataset, 2026] Postings that closed in the 60 days to 2026-09-09, across 10 boards: 3.8% closed within 7 days and 54.4% within 30 days. Median days live by board (75th percentile in brackets): Lever 8 (10), Paylocity 13 (14), Ashby 14 (33), Greenhouse 14 (36), Indeed 30 (35), LinkedIn 30 (36), Y Combinator 33 (47). Share closed within 7 days: Lever 43.8%, Ashby 20.4%, Greenhouse 20.5%, Indeed 8.4%, LinkedIn <0.5%. Share closed within 30 days: Lever 93.0%, Greenhouse 68.1%, Ashby 69.6%, LinkedIn 51.5%. — [JobsPipe, Job posting lifespan](https://jobspipe.dev/job-posting-lifespan.md)
- Same source, caveat: LinkedIn and Indeed closures are detected at the 30-day listing expiry, so their medians "reflect that expiry rule rather than employer behavior". Days live is measured from JobsPipe's first sighting to the close. No sample sizes are published, and there is no breakdown by seniority, internship or country. 28.6% of active postings were first seen more than 30 days earlier, and 8.0% reappeared after a gap of 10 or more days (reposts). — [JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/job-posting-lifespan)
- Same source, survival among ATS postings: the share still open at 14 and 30 days was 35.0% and 27.1% for Greenhouse, 71.6% and 69.5% for Ashby, and 66.0% and 56.7% for Lever. — [JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/job-posting-lifespan.md). (These survival cohorts look inconsistent with the median figures for Ashby and Lever. Treat them as rough.)
- [STUDY, 2017; US professional and tech roles] DHI Group data covering about 80M applications and about 10M postings: single-position postings had a **mean duration of 9.8 days**, about one quarter of mean vacancy duration in JOLTS. — [St. Louis Fed, How Quickly Do People Apply for Jobs? (2017)](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2017/november/how-quickly-people-apply-jobs), based on Davis and Samaniego de la Parra, "Application Flows" (2017 working paper)
- [STUDY, NL, old] Indeed NL, 2017: **27.6% of Dutch vacancies were still open after 60 days**. By city: Amsterdam 28.6%, Rotterdam 29.4%, Utrecht 28.3%, Den Haag 27.4%. — [HRpraktijk / Indeed (10 Oct 2017)](https://www.hrpraktijk.nl/werving-selectie/recruitment/meer-dan-een-kwart-van-vacatures-staat-langer-dan-60-dagen-open/)

**Platform rules (LinkedIn)**
- [PLATFORM RULE] A free LinkedIn job post is active for **up to 14 days** and is then paused. If not promoted, it **closes automatically after 30 days**. Free posts have an **application limit of typically 10 to 30 applications** depending on the role. When the limit is hit, the post is paused and removed from search. One free post can be active at a time. — [LinkedIn Help: Post a free job](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a517777)
- [PLATFORM RULE, ATS sync] Jobs syncing from SAP SuccessFactors to LinkedIn expire at 30 days and must be reposted, which creates a new URL. — [SAP KB 2787433](https://userapps.support.sap.com/sap/support/knowledge/en/2787433) (from search snippet only)
- [ANECDOTE] LinkedIn's public applicant label tops out at "Over 100 applicants". — [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/over-100-applicants/)

**Application arrival curve**
- [STUDY, 2017] **41% of applications arrive within the first 48 hours and 56% within the first 96 hours** of posting. Applications per posting fall sharply as a posting ages. — [St. Louis Fed (2017)](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2017/november/how-quickly-people-apply-jobs)
- [VENDOR/ANECDOTE] Indeed career advice says that for some jobs "the bulk" of applications arrive in the first 48 to 72 hours. No source is given. — [Indeed career advice](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/news/indeed-expert-reasons-employers-not-responding)

**Time-to-fill and time-to-hire**
- [VENDOR, large dataset] Ashby (7 May 2026): more than 100M applications and 200k jobs over 5 years. Open roles average **more than 300 applications**, and applications per hire have **tripled since 2021**. Candidates are **about 50% less likely to reach interview** than five years ago. Time to first fill is **about 8 weeks for business roles and about 10 weeks for technical roles**. — [HR Dive on Ashby (13 May 2026)](https://www.hrdive.com/news/recruiters-see-job-applications-triple-to-more-than-300-per-role/820096/)
- [VENDOR, secondary, unverified] A summary of Ashby's 2026 data gives median time to fill of **52 days for junior roles and 75 days for technical roles**, and interviews scheduled in about 4 days on average. I could not verify this against Ashby's own publication. — [Stealth Agents summary](https://stealthagents.com/research/recruiting-interview-scheduling-workload-statistics-2026)
- [STUDY/VENDOR] Indeed Hiring Lab, as cited by Indeed: median time to hire rose **from 20 days (early 2023) to 32 days (March 2026)** across occupations. — [Indeed career advice](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/news/indeed-expert-reasons-employers-not-responding) (from search summary)
- [VENDOR, user-tracked data] Huntr Q2 2026: **median of 6 days from application to interview** and 10 days from interview to offer. Q1 2025: 75% of those who hear back do so within 15 days, and 90% within 38 days. — [Huntr Q2 2026](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q2-2026); [Huntr Q1 2025](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q1-2025)
- [VENDOR, NL] MrWork Recruitment Trends (2024 data): the general Dutch time-to-fill benchmark was **45 days**, while respondents averaged 22 days (agencies 18.4, SMEs 20.5, corporates 30+). Intelligence Group (2024), as cited secondhand: average NL time-to-fill **42 days**, with the best quartile at 28 days or less. — [recruitmenttraining.pro, time-to-fill](https://recruitmenttraining.pro/nieuws/time-to-fill/); [recruitmenttraining.pro, time-to-hire](https://recruitmenttraining.pro/nieuws/time-to-hire-verlagen/)
- [ANECDOTE] A blog attributes an NL average time-to-hire of **36 working days** to Intelligence Group 2026. Unverified. — [Searchlab](https://searchlab.nl/statistieken/recruitment-marketing-statistieken-2026)

### Inferences
- For modelling, use the ATS close as the "true" end of a posting. LinkedIn and Indeed age is capped and censored by the 30-day expiry, so a 25-day-old LinkedIn post may already be closed on the employer side.
- A reasonable prior for posting lifetime is a median of about 14 days with a long tail (75th percentile about 33 to 36 days) on Greenhouse and Ashby, and shorter for Lever-style or SME postings.
- The arrival curve is front-loaded. By about day 4, roughly half of eventual applicants are already in. A late applicant's "pile" is therefore mostly fixed, but they arrive after screening may already have started (see section 2).
- Ashby's 52-day junior time-to-fill set against a 14-day median posting life suggests postings often come down while selection continues. For a late applicant this points to batch decisions on an already-collected pool.

### Gaps
- I found no internship or entry-level split of posting duration, and no NL or EU split, in any dataset (JobsPipe has no seniority or country cut).
- I found no Indeed Hiring Lab figures on the daily share of applications. The 41%/56% curve is US data from 2017 (DHI, tech and professional roles).
- I found no current official doc giving promoted LinkedIn job duration. The help page does not state it.
- I found no NL time-to-first-interview benchmark.

## 2. Rolling review vs batch review; evidence that early applicants get more interviews

### Takeaway
The best-known "apply early" numbers are weakly sourced. TalentWorks' "8x in the first 96 hours" comes from about 1,600 applications around 2017, with an unpublished method. GoApply's "2 to 3x" is a vendor claim. Huntr publishes no cut by days since posting. The causal evidence is thin, but the platform data (front-loaded arrivals, short ATS lifetimes) and recruiter accounts both point to rolling or early screening being the norm in the private sector. Dutch public and university employers more often review after the closing date.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR, about 2017] TalentWorks: applying within **96 hours** of posting gives **up to 8x** interview odds, with chances reported to fall about **28% per day** after that. The analysis is based on about **1,600 applications**. The original report was not retrievable. Secondary summaries disagree on the window (some say days 2 to 4 are best, another says "first 24 h gives 6x vs waiting 4 days"). — [paulvanderlaken.com summary (2017)](https://paulvanderlaken.com/2017/10/24/talent-works-data-science-to-improve-job-application-chances/); [CSUN career blog (2024)](https://nazariancareers.csun.edu/blog/2024/12/24/when-should-you-apply-for-a-job-heres-how-to-8x-your-odds/) (cites only "TalentWorks Inc", with no method)
- [VENDOR] GoApply (2024, more than 10,000 job seekers): applying within **24 to 48 h gave 2 to 3x more interviews** than waiting a week or more, and "72% of offers went to people who applied within the first 5 days". The method is unpublished and the company sells an application tool. — [GoApply blog](https://www.goapply.ai/blog/best-time-to-apply)
- [ANECDOTE] A "Ladders survey" is cited as saying the best interview odds come from applying within **72 hours**. I did not find the primary source. — cited in [TopResume](https://topresume.com/career-advice/the-early-bird-gets-the-job-the-best-time-to-submit-your-resume)
- [ANECDOTE] "Jobvite (2019): some employers only consider the first ~50% (30 of 62) of applications that pass the ATS". This is secondhand and I could not verify it. — cited in search results summarising [Career Edge](https://www.careeredge.ca/timing-everything-apply-early-arrive-early/) and similar blogs
- [VENDOR] Huntr's quarterly reports give interview rates by number of applications sent (Q1 2026: users with 11 to 20 applications had a **9.25%** interview rate per application, vs **2.58%** for users with 100 or more) and by board, but **no cut by days since posting**. — [Huntr Q1 2026](https://huntr.co/research/job-search-trends-q1-2026)
- [ANECDOTE] A career blog claims most teams start reviewing within **24 to 72 h** of posting, and that a recruiter may shortlist strong candidates from the first **50 to 100** applicants and contact them within days. No source is given. — [InterviewPal](https://www.interviewpal.com/blog/why-early-job-applications-get-more-attention-and-what-that-actually-means-for-you)
- [ANECDOTE] Secondhand claims, unverified: "LinkedIn report: applicants in the first 10% of the pool are 4x as likely to get an interview", and "Robert Half: most hiring managers decide within two weeks of posting". I could not locate the primary reports.
- [STUDY, academic, adjacent] Median resume review time is about **31 seconds** (Lerner 2024), as cited in an arXiv hiring paper. This concerns attention per resume, not order effects. — [Hiring as Exploration, arXiv 2411.03616](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03616) (via search summary)
- [PLATFORM RULE / NL public sector] Radboud Universiteit commits to replying **"uiterlijk binnen vier weken na sluiting van de vacature"** (at the latest within four weeks after the vacancy closes). The response clock is tied to the closing date, which is consistent with batch review. The page does not say whether review is rolling. — [Radboud Universiteit, sollicitatieprocedure](https://www.ru.nl/werken-bij/sollicitatieprocedure)

### Inferences
- No credible study isolates the causal effect of applying earlier. The 8x figure is confounded: early applicants may be better matched or more motivated, and late applications include those that arrive after the role is effectively filled.
- In the private sector and on ATS sites with short lifetimes (Lever median 8 days), screening is effectively rolling, so late applicants compete only with whoever remains in the unreviewed or undecided queue, plus a shortlist that may already be formed.
- For Dutch public bodies, universities and postings that state a "sluitingsdatum" (closing date), batch review after close is more plausible. There, applying on day 1 or on the last day makes little difference, and every applicant competes with the whole pile.
- A practical app rule: if the posting has an explicit closing date and comes from a public or academic employer, treat it as batch, so the competitor set is the projected total pile. If it is private, an ATS posting or one with no closing date, treat it as rolling, so the competitor set is the applicants who arrived before you plus a decaying share of those after. Weight the competitor set against interview probability.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the original TalentWorks, Ladders, Jobvite or LinkedIn "first 10%" reports. All numbers are secondhand.
- I found no Teal report on application timing.
- I found no survey measuring the share of employers that use rolling vs batch review, globally or in NL.
- I found no peer-reviewed callback study (audit or field experiment) that varies application timing.

## 3. Pile caps ("we review the first N") and early closure

### Takeaway
The only hard, documented cap is a platform one: LinkedIn free posts pause at about 10 to 30 applications. Recruiter-side "stop after N" behaviour is supported only by anecdotes and quotes. Early closure is common in practice: about 20% of Greenhouse and Ashby postings and about 44% of Lever postings close within 7 days. Dutch postings often carry "vacature kan eerder sluiten" language, but I could not document how often this happens.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM RULE] LinkedIn free posts: application limit of **10 to 30 applications**, after which the post is **paused and removed from search**. — [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a517777)
- [STUDY, aggregator] Share of postings closed within 7 days: **Lever 43.8%, Ashby 20.4%, Greenhouse 20.5%** (all boards combined: 3.8%, which is distorted by LinkedIn and Indeed expiry). — [JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/job-posting-lifespan.md)
- [ANECDOTE] Recruiter quotes in HR press, as relayed: when a pile reaches 500 to 1,000, "the process would simply stop after a few days", and recruiters start interviewing once strong candidates appear in the first batch. — via search summary of [Enhancv recruiter roundup](https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/) and the [Quora thread](https://www.quora.com/Do-job-recruiters-stop-reviewing-candidate-profiles-after-reading-a-certain-amount-of-resumes-Are-the-recruiters-obligated-to-read-them-all-I-am-concerned-that-I-will-be-wasting-my-time-applying-to-a-position-with)
- [ANECDOTE] A practitioner post says recruiters commonly sort by most recent, read from the top and stop once interview slots are filled. — [Medium, "Is High Application Volume Quietly Killing Your Hiring Speed?"](https://medium.com/@unblit/is-high-application-volume-quietly-killing-your-hiring-speed-776dd340e5b7)
- [VENDOR] With more than 300 applications per role on average (Ashby 2026), recruiter capacity is about 7 hires per recruiter per quarter. This is consistent with incomplete review of large piles. — [HR Dive / Ashby](https://www.hrdive.com/news/recruiters-see-job-applications-triple-to-more-than-300-per-role/820096/)

### Inferences
- Model an effective review cap rather than a hard N. When the projected pile is far above about 100 to 300, assume applicants after the point where a shortlist forms get sharply discounted attention.
- On LinkedIn free posts, a post that is visible but has collected fewer than about 10 to 30 applicants is still accepting applications. Once it hits the limit it vanishes from search, so the visible competitor count of a free post is capped by construction.
- Early closure (within a week) is common enough on ATS postings (20 to 44%) that a posting seen on day 10 or later has survived selection pressure. Either it is hard to fill, or it is a "ghost" or evergreen posting (8% reappear after a gap).

### Gaps
- I found no survey showing the share of recruiters who stop at a fixed N, and no data on how often NL vacancies close before the stated closing date.

## 4. Dutch practices: stage cycles, graduate deadlines, closing-date norms

### Takeaway
Dutch internship (stage) recruitment is tied to the academic calendar and is often mediated by the school. Universities and colleges advise starting early but publish no fixed lead time. Big-company traineeships (e.g., Unilever UFLP NL) use short fixed application windows of about 3 weeks, but reportedly fill places first-come, first-served within them, so early applicants matter even inside a window. Public and university vacancies tie their responses to the closing date.

### Cited Findings
- Unilever Future Leaders Programme NL: application windows of about 3 weeks were seen in search snippets ("March 1st to March 21st" and "March 6th to March 27th"), for a 1 September start (2024 cycle). The same page reportedly says **spots are filled on a first-come, first-served basis**. My direct fetch of the current page did not show these details, so they are from search snippets and unverified for 2026. — [Unilever careers NL-UFLP](https://careers.unilever.com/NL-UFLP)
- A UK guide lists Unilever's scheme among those with **rolling deadlines** (UK, 2024). — [Bright Network](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/application-deadlines/jobs/graduate-schemes/consumer-fmcg-retail/)
- Utrecht University (several programmes): students are responsible for finding their own stage, and should "begin op tijd" (start in time) because searching and applying takes a long time. No months are specified. — [UU students: stage](https://students.uu.nl/gw/las/mijn-studie/vrije-ruimte/stage)
- Some hogescholen (HBO) run internal internship vacancy banks where the stagebureau (internship office) assigns the period first, and the student then applies by email with a letter and CV. This is a school-gated cycle. — [HHS Stagewerving 2025-2026 (huidtherapie)](https://huidtherapie.nl/app/uploads/HHS-Stagewerving-2025-2026.pdf)
- MBO: students should search on stagemarkt.nl for recognised learning companies (erkende leerbedrijven), and schools run stagemarkten (internship fairs). — [JobMBO Stagewijzer](https://jobmbo.nl/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Stagewijzer.pdf)
- A Magnet.me example of a NL entry-level vacancy, "Startende marktonderzoeker" at Intelligence Group, was posted 24 July 2026 with a **deadline of 1 August 2026**, a window of about 8 days. — [Magnet.me vacancy](https://magnet.me/nl-NL/vacature/1062219/startende-marktonderzoeker) (from search snippet)
- Radboud: response within four weeks after the vacancy closes. — [Radboud](https://www.ru.nl/werken-bij/sollicitatieprocedure)

### Inferences
- For NL student and graduate apps, there are two regimes. **Windowed** postings (traineeships, Magnet.me deadlines, public and academic) have a known close, and the pile is mostly reviewed in a batch, unless the employer says first-come-first-served, as Unilever reportedly does. **Open** postings (company ATS, LinkedIn) behave like rolling review.
- Magnet.me deadlines are short (about 1 to 2 weeks in the example), so a projected pile for windowed NL entry-level roles should be integrated only up to the deadline.

### Gaps
- I found no published Dutch data on stage lead times, for example "X months before the start", or on peak stage posting months.
- I could not confirm current Philips, Shell or ING traineeship deadlines or whether they review on a rolling basis.
- I found no Intelligence Group or Werf& study on closing-date norms or on rolling vs batch screening in NL.
- The Indeed NL figures on open duration are from 2017. I found nothing newer that was public.
