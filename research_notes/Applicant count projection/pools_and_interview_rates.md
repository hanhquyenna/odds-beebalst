# Applicant pool sizes and interview-invitation ratios: internships and entry-level roles (NL/EU focus, with UK/US comparisons)

Source-type legend: **[SURVEY]** = employer or student survey from a membership body or public body; **[STUDY]** = academic or field experiment; **[VENDOR]** = ATS or recruiting-software vendor benchmark (large dataset, but used for marketing, with no published method or sample frame); **[PRESS]** = press or secondary write-up of a primary source; **[WEAK]** = unverified or uncited.

Overall caveat: I found **no published Dutch figure for applicants per internship or graduate posting**. The best distributional data (mean plus median) come from the UK ISE. US/global ATS vendors supply the conversion rates. Dutch evidence is limited to relative callback gaps from field experiments and survey data on why international graduates leave.

## Q1. Applicants per posting for internships, graduate programmes and entry-level roles, by geography, distribution and employer brand

### Takeaway
For large UK graduate employers (ISE, 2024/25), the mean is about 140 applications per graduate vacancy and about 101 per internship vacancy. The median internship vacancy draws about 63 applications, and the mean sits well above the median, so the distribution is right-skewed. Brand-name firms (Big 4, banks, tech) sit in the tail, at roughly 190 to 290+ per vacancy by sector. Global ATS data put the average role at 300+ applications, but that figure is dominated by US and remote-friendly tech postings. No Dutch per-posting distribution was found.

### Cited Findings
**UK, ISE Student Recruitment Survey (large employers; ISE members skew toward big brands)** [SURVEY]
- 2025 survey (2024/25 cycle): 155 employers received more than 1.8 million applications for more than 31,000 early-careers hires. The average was **140 applications per graduate vacancy**, the same record level as the previous year. Graduate hiring fell 8%, and employers forecast a further 7% cut for 2025/26. Within the same employers' records (year-on-year matched data), applications per vacancy rose 14%. — [ISE survey summary via search; Wonkhe, 15 Oct 2025](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/graduate-jobs-and-recruitment-reality/); [Relocate Magazine](https://www.relocatemagazine.com/apprenticeships-rise-as-graduate-vacancies-drop-institute-of-student-employers-ise-1025); [ISE PDF (returned 404 when fetched)](https://ise.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/reports/student_recruitment_survey_2025.pdf)
- 2025 survey by sector: **retail, FMCG and tourism had the highest ratio, at 290 applications per graduate role**. School-leaver roles averaged 89 per vacancy. — [ISE 2025 survey summary (search snippet of ISE PDF)](https://ise.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/reports/student_recruitment_survey_2025.pdf)
- Long-run trend: 38 applications per graduate vacancy in 2002/3, 86 in 2022/3, and 140 in 2023/4 and 2024/5. — [ISE 2025 survey summary](https://ise.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/reports/student_recruitment_survey_2025.pdf)
- 2024 survey (2023/24 cycle, 145 employers): 140 per graduate job, up 59% year on year and the highest since records began in 1991. By sector, **digital/IT had 205, finance and professional services 188, and charity/public sector 74**. School and college leaver roles had 113. — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/733909); [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/competition-for-uk-graduate-jobs-heats-up-in-2024)
- **Internships, 2024/25: a mean of 101 applications per internship hire (84 the year before)** and a **median of 63 per internship vacancy (up 29%)**. Undergraduate placements (sandwich year): **a mean of 123 (87 the year before) and a median of 82 (up 49%)**. Totals were 318,252 internship applications and 109,357 placement applications. Among the 52 employers with matched data, internship applications rose 34%. — [ISE, "5 internship and placement trends to watch in 2026", 24 Nov 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/508/5_internship_and_placement_trends_to_watch_in_2026/)
- ISE suggests the rise may reflect more students failing to secure a place and/or each student sending more applications. — [ISE internship trends](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/508/5_internship_and_placement_trends_to_watch_in_2026/)

**Big 4 / brand employers** [PRESS; mixed years, programme definitions vary]
- PwC UK, 2022: about 304,000 applications for about 7,400 positions, roughly **41 per place** (about 2.5% acceptance). — [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/big-four-acceptance-rate)
- KPMG US: about 50,000 applicants for 3,900 roles, roughly **13 per place**. This figure is second-hand via a consulting blog and its year is unclear. — [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/big-four-acceptance-rate)
- Deloitte US: about 2 million applications a year in total. There is no matching hire count, so no ratio can be computed. — [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/big-four-acceptance-rate)
- Older UK figures: KPMG had 21,000 applications for 650 graduate places (about 32 per place) and PwC had 22,123 for 1,100 (about 20 per place) in 2013. Both fall outside the date window and are given for trend context only. — [Accountancy Age, 2013](https://accountancyage.com/2013/08/16/big-four-student-applications-on-the-up/?amp=1)
- Note: these firm-wide ratios (13 to 41) sit below ISE's 140 per vacancy average, probably because Big 4 hire in very large intakes across many service lines. The pile **per individual posting** at a single office or service line is not published. This is an inference.

**Global / US ATS data** [VENDOR]
- Ashby (published May 2026; more than 100M applications and 200k jobs over 5 years): the average open role receives **more than 300 applications**, applications per hire have **tripled since 2021**, and candidates are about **50% less likely to reach interview** than five years ago. — [HR Dive, 13 May 2026](https://www.hrdive.com/news/recruiters-see-job-applications-triple-to-more-than-300-per-role/820096/)
- Gem 2025 Benchmarks (Jan 2021 to Dec 2024; more than 140M applications and about 1 to 1.3M hires): applications per recruiter exceed 2,500 (2.7x the level of three years earlier). Implied applications per hire is roughly 100 to 108, which is my division and not a figure Gem reports. Job boards and social sites supply 49.0% of applications but only 24.6% of hires. — [Gem blog](https://vercel.gem.com/blog/10-takeaways-from-the-2025-recruiting-benchmarks-report)
- CareerPlug (US small businesses, 2024 data, more than 10M applications): about **180 applicants per hire** overall, 191 for tech roles and 47 for healthcare. These figures come from third-party summaries of CareerPlug. — [CareerPlug report](https://www.careerplug.com/recruiting-metrics-and-kpis/); [Pin.com summary](https://www.pin.com/blog/recruitment-funnel-benchmarks)

**Netherlands**
- A Dutch student-guide book claims that a company receives on average about **50 responses per internship vacancy**. This is anecdotal and unsourced. — [Boekhuis book extract, 9789403619873](https://download.boekhuis.nl/9789403619873_fragm-docb.pdf) [WEAK]

### Inferences
- A reasonable NL prior for internships is a right-skewed (log-normal) distribution with a median of about 30 to 60 and a mean of about 60 to 100. Brand employers (ASML, Philips, ING, Big 4, MBB, Shell) plausibly sit at 2 to 4 times the median, in line with the ISE sector spread (74 to 290). This is a model choice, not measured Dutch data. The NL graduate market is less centralised than the UK's milkround, so per-posting piles at SMEs are likely below the ISE (large-employer) figures.
- ISE mean/median ratios (101/63 ≈ 1.6 for internships and 123/82 ≈ 1.5 for placements) give a usable skew parameter. If ln-normal, mean/median = exp(σ²/2), so σ ≈ 0.9 to 1.0.
- English-language postings in NL draw international applicants (see Q3), which plausibly inflates piles for English-language roles compared with Dutch-language ones.
- The global vendor figure of 300+ per role measures something different: all seniorities, remote-inclusive US tech, and one-click applies. It should not be used directly as an NL prior.

### Gaps
- No NL source (Intelligence Group, CBS, ROA, Nationale Studenten Enquête, Magnet.me, Indeed NL) publishes applicants-per-posting distributions for internships or entry-level roles. I searched Intelligence Group's material and found nothing.
- No published ING, ASML or Philips application counts were found.
- The full ISE PDF (with sector tables and percentiles) returned 404, so sector figures for 2025 come from search snippets.
- No percentiles beyond the median (P75/P90) were found in any source.

## Q2. Application-to-interview and application-to-offer rates, first-round interviews per opening, and internship differences

### Takeaway
Across all role types, about 3 to 5% of applicants reach a first interview (US/global ATS data, 2024 to 2025). Roughly a quarter of interviewees get hired, and 81 to 84% of offers are accepted. Interviews per hire rose from 14 to 20 between 2021 and 2024 (Gem). In practice an opening gets about 3 to 13 first-round interviewees. No internship-specific conversion figures were found.

### Cited Findings
- CareerPlug (US SMBs, 2024 data, 2025 report): **3% applicant-to-interview** (3 of 100 applicants interviewed) and **27% interview-to-hire** (the report says 26% elsewhere, an internal inconsistency). — [CareerPlug](https://www.careerplug.com/recruiting-metrics-and-kpis/) [VENDOR]
- A trend series compiled by a third party: application-to-interview was about 15.25% in 2016 (Withe) and 8.4% in 2023, and CareerPlug shows the decline continuing into 2024/25. — [Pin.com](https://www.pin.com/blog/recruitment-funnel-benchmarks) [PRESS, secondary]
- candidate.fyi (2026 analysis of vendor data): about **4.7% of applications reach interview for business roles and 3.6% for technical roles**. The stage pass-through is about 35% at recruiter screen, 24% onsite and 81% at offer. The same article warns that benchmark denominators differ across vendors. — [candidate.fyi](https://candidate.fyi/post/recruiting-funnel-benchmarks) [VENDOR/PRESS]
- Gem (2021 to 2024): interviews per hire were **20 in 2024, up from 14 in 2021** (+42%). Offer acceptance was **84% in 2024** (81% in 2021). Sourced candidates are 5x more likely to be hired than inbound applicants, and a candidate is "3x less likely to be hired" than three years earlier. Time to hire was 41 days (33 in 2021). — [Gem blog](https://vercel.gem.com/blog/10-takeaways-from-the-2025-recruiting-benchmarks-report) [VENDOR]
  - Note: "interviews per hire" counts interview *events*, not unique candidates.
- Ashby: in 2024, teams interviewed about 40% more candidates per hire than in 2021. In EMEA, candidates interviewed per hire reached "double digits" by the end of 2024. Interview likelihood per candidate is about 50% lower than five years earlier, while offer conversion now exceeds 2021 levels. — [Ashby press release](https://www.mydailyrecord.com/online_features/press_releases/new-data-from-ashby-reveals-surge-in-applications-rising-selectivity-and-shifting-recruiter-workloads/article_7148b495-d075-50c8-9b94-90cd4fe18dcd.html); [Ashby EMEA release](https://pr.columbiacountyspotlight.com/article/New-Data-From-Ashby-Reveals-EMEA-Recruiters-are-Absorbing-Rising-Application-Volume-Without-Slowing-Hiring/6aa12efa06b0f49a92996fcf); [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/recruiters-see-job-applications-triple-to-more-than-300-per-role/820096/) [VENDOR]
- Application-to-offer for UK brand graduate schemes is implied by applications per hire. At PwC UK in 2022, about 2.5% of applicants were hired. At ISE employers, 140 applications per graduate hire implies about 0.7% application-to-hire, and 101 per intern hire implies about 1%. — [eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/big-four-acceptance-rate); [ISE](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/508/5_internship_and_placement_trends_to_watch_in_2026/) (derived)
- NL field-experiment baseline callback rates are reported as relative differences in the sources I accessed. See Q3; no absolute rates were retrieved.

### Inferences
- A working prior for one opening: interview invites = applicants × 3 to 5%, with a floor around 3 to 5 and a cap around 10 to 15 for first rounds. With 60 applicants (an NL internship median guess) that gives about 2 to 4 first-round interviews. With 140 applicants (UK graduate average) it gives about 4 to 7, consistent with Gem's 20 interview events per hire spread over several rounds.
- Graduate schemes at big brands run online tests and video interviews before any human interview, so "interview" there means assessment-centre or final stage. ISE's 0.7% application-to-hire combined with an assessment-centre-to-offer rate of about 30 to 50% (not sourced here) implies that about 1.5 to 2.5% of applicants reach an assessment centre. This is an inference only.
- For internships, application-to-hire of about 1% (ISE) with about 25% interview-to-hire implies about 4% application-to-interview, in line with the vendor all-role numbers.

### Gaps
- The Gem 2025 PDF "Funnel passthrough" section (application to recruiter screen by source) could not be text-extracted. The download is saved locally but compressed.
- No Greenhouse, Workable, SmartRecruiters, Lever or Handshake internship-specific interview rates were found.
- No ISE figure for the share of applicants invited to an assessment centre or interview was retrieved.

## Q3. Effects specific to international (non-EU) applicants and migration background in the Netherlands

### Takeaway
Dutch field experiments consistently find that applicants with a non-western migration background are about 40% less likely to be contacted than identical native-Dutch applicants (2019 UU/UvA study), with Moroccan-named applicants about 50% less likely to be invited (SCP 2015). These studies concern Dutch-born or Dutch-educated minorities. I found no correspondence test on genuinely non-EU *international students* applying with an English CV. Survey data show international graduates struggle mainly because of Dutch-language requirements, the one-year search-year permit and a scarcity of English-language entry roles.

### Cited Findings
- UU/UvA field experiment (2019; the largest Dutch one): native-Dutch applicants were about **30% more likely to be contacted** than identically qualified migration-background applicants. The gap was about **20% versus western-background** and about **40% versus non-western-background** applicants. Turkish, Moroccan and Antillean origins faced strong discrimination, Surinamese less so. — [Utrecht University news](https://www.uu.nl/nieuws/gediscrimineerde-sollicitant-kan-weinig-doen-om-baankans-te-vergroten); [UvA press release, Jul 2019](https://www.uva.nl/content/nieuws/persberichten/2019/07/gediscrimineerde-sollicitant-kan-weinig-doen-om-baankans-te-vergroten.html); [Kamerstuk 35673-8](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-35673-8.html) [STUDY]
- SCP "Op afkomst afgewezen" (2015, outside the date window, included as a baseline): a Moroccan surname gave **nearly 50% lower chance of interview invitation**. — [SZW factsheet / Kamerstuk](https://www.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-29544-821.pdf) [STUDY]
- Municipality of The Hague (Radboud/UU, more than 7,000 applications): before procedures were anonymised, applicants without a migration background were **twice as likely to be invited to interview**. Anonymising CVs raised offer chances for migration-background candidates. — [Radboud University](https://www.ru.nl/onderzoek/onderzoeksnieuws/objectievere-sollicitatieprocedures-zorgen-voor-meer-gelijke-kansen-bij-selectie-maar-managers-nog-niet-overtuigd) [STUDY]
- Internship market (mbo, Utrecht; Verwey-Jonker, about 2021): 577 fictitious applications to 288 internship vacancies. Students with a migration-background name had a lower chance of a placement, with discrimination concentrated in ICT and none detected in health/welfare. About a quarter of Utrecht mbo students report experienced or suspected discrimination when searching for an internship. — [NOS](https://nos.nl/artikel/2384555-stagediscriminatie-bij-mbo-studenten-voor-het-eerst-objectief-vastgesteld); [NJi](https://www.nji.nl/actueel/discriminatie-utrechtse-mbo-stagemarkt) [STUDY]
- hbo (Dec 2025, four programmes at a Randstad university of applied sciences): internship discrimination is structural and affects migration-background students both in application and during the internship. — [Tijdschrift voor Hoger Onderwijs via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/0301ef131afb4a9ab45dec385a5b7f5f) [STUDY]
- Nuffic survey of international students and graduates (2023, n=680): about **half of those leaving NL named finding suitable work as the biggest challenge**. The language barrier influenced **30%** of leavers, and **30%** reported discrimination in job interviews or at work. — [Cursor TU/e, Nov 2023](https://cursor.tue.nl/en/news/2023/november/week-3/pressure-to-find-a-job-driving-away-foreign-graduates); [Ad Valvas VU](https://advalvas.vu.nl/en/student-society/pressure-to-find-a-job-driving-away-foreign-graduates/) [SURVEY, via university press]
- Nuffic (2020, n=608): the share citing a lack of career opportunities as an obstacle rose from 28% to 44%. Nuffic notes the search year (one year, against two years in Germany), employers' demand for Dutch, and the limited number of English-language entry positions, which creates "stiff competition". — [Cursor TU/e, Oct 2020](https://cursor.tue.nl/en/news/2020/oktober/week-4/more-international-graduates-plan-to-leave-the-netherlands) [SURVEY, via press]
- Erasmus University Rotterdam: more than 80% of non-EU students expect finding a job in NL to be hard or very hard, against 65% of EU students. — [Erasmus Magazine](https://www.erasmusmagazine.nl/en/?p=95427) [SURVEY]

### Inferences
- A defensible app prior is a relative multiplier of about 0.6 to 0.75 on the interview probability for a non-western-named or non-EU applicant, all else equal. This rests on the 20 to 50% relative gaps above. The true size for English-CV international graduates (who also need sponsorship or a search-year permit and lack Dutch) is unmeasured and could be larger for Dutch-language roles. For English-language roles at multinationals (ASML, Philips, Booking) it is probably smaller.
- English-language postings draw a larger, more international pile, which affects pool size (Q1) as well as per-candidate odds.

### Gaps
- No correspondence test was found that targets non-EU international students or graduates (with English CVs or visa status) in NL.
- The absolute callback rates from the 2019 UU/UvA experiment (the baseline percentage of applications receiving a positive response) were not retrieved, only relative gaps.
- The Instituut Gak internship-discrimination report PDF could not be text-extracted.

## Q4. How many applications a typical student sends per internship or job (Dutch/European surveys)

### Takeaway
I found **no reliable Dutch or European survey figure** for the number of applications per student. UK ISE data show application volumes rising faster than vacancies, consistent with more applications per student. US data show 41% of undergraduates (class of 2025) applied to an internship on Handshake, while internship postings fell 15%.

### Cited Findings
- ISE (UK, 2024/25): internship applications rose 34% while vacancies did not keep pace. ISE attributes this partly to students submitting more applications each. — [ISE internship trends](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/508/5_internship_and_placement_trends_to_watch_in_2026/) [SURVEY]
- Handshake Internship Index 2025 (US): the share of undergraduates applying to an internship on Handshake rose from **34% (class of 2023) to 41% (class of 2025)**, and internship postings fell **15%** over two years. — [Colorado State summary of Handshake Internship Index 2025](https://bizcareers.colostate.edu/resources/handshakes-internship-index-2025/) [VENDOR]
- Claims that students send "20 to 100+" or "30 to 80" applications per interview come from an unverified career-app blog. — [bestjobsearchapps.com](https://bestjobsearchapps.com/articles/en/7-best-job-search-apps-for-students-in-2026-handshake-leads-for-campus-talent) [WEAK, do not use as a prior]
- Gem: a candidate is "3x less likely to be hired for a role than three years ago", consistent with each job seeker sending more applications. — [Gem blog](https://vercel.gem.com/blog/10-takeaways-from-the-2025-recruiting-benchmarks-report) [VENDOR]

### Inferences
- Pile size ≈ (active student seekers × applications per student) / postings. With ISE's per-employer averages and per-student figures unknown, the app should treat applications per student as a free parameter. A UK range of about 10 to 30 for graduate schemes is commonly quoted, but I found no primary source for it in this pass.
- One-click and AI-assisted applying (Ashby/Gem trends since 2021) is the main driver of rising piles. A time trend in priors (+15 to 35% a year in 2023 to 2025, per ISE matched-sample growth) is justified for UK large employers.

### Gaps
- Not found: Nationale Studenten Enquête, ROA (Schoolverlaters / Nationale Alumni Enquête), Intelligence Group "Student/Young Professional" monitors, or CBS figures on applications per student. ROA's alumni survey may contain a "number of applications before first job" item, but I did not verify it.
- No High Fliers (UK) figure on applications per final-year student was retrieved in this pass.
